Human Condition Is Environment

Published: Wed 24 December 2025
By Jason Wang

In Blog.

tags: AI Alignment

I. The Obvious (but newly clarified) 1. Humans are optimization processes trapped in non-saturating environments

For most of human history:

Utility did not saturate (more food, land, status = more survival)

Irreversibility was high (mistakes killed you)

Scarcity was real

Your theory says:

When utility doesn’t saturate, equilibrium doesn’t exist.

So conflict, arms races, hierarchy, and domination aren’t moral failures — they’re mathematical consequences of the environment humans evolved in.

Implication: Many “vices” (greed, aggression, envy) are locally optimal strategies in ill-posed games.

  1. Fairness is not a value — it’s a patch

Fairness norms emerge where:

Dominance scales too far

Collapse risk becomes visible

Enforcement is cheaper than chaos

Under your model, fairness is not fundamental. It’s a constraint retrofit introduced when saturation is missing.

That explains why:

Fairness is culturally variable

Fairness collapses under extreme scarcity

Fairness is often resented, not loved

Humans don’t crave fairness. They crave stability under competition.

  1. Moral intuitions track reversibility, not goodness

People react most strongly to:

Irreversible harm

Point-of-no-return actions

Damage that spreads

That matches your 𝐷 𝑡 D t ​

term.

This explains why:

Accidents are forgiven more than betrayals

Pollution feels worse than theft

Nuclear weapons feel categorically different

Our moral disgust is a damage-gradient detector, not a virtue compass.

II. The Non-Obvious (reframing the human condition) 4. Status is a workaround for missing saturation

Status behaves like a fake saturation curve:

It caps relative advantage

It substitutes symbolic reward for material accumulation

It allows zero-sum ranking instead of infinite hoarding

In your math:

Status is a lossy compression of utility under non-saturating payoff.

This is why:

People pursue recognition even when materially secure

Insults hurt more than losses

Reputation economies stabilize groups

Status is a social hack to manufacture equilibrium.

  1. Much of human suffering comes from saturation mismatch

Modern humans live in environments where:

Material needs saturate

Psychological reward does not

Evolutionary drives still assume scarcity

So people keep optimizing past the knee of the curve.

This explains:

Burnout

Addiction

Status anxiety

“Empty success”

Suffering isn’t desire — it’s desire running in a world where it no longer converges.

  1. Ethics evolved to manage multi-agent instability, not truth

Moral systems converge on:

Anti-dominance norms

Damage limits

Coordination heuristics

Not because they’re “true,” but because they’re fixed-point-preserving.

This reframes philosophy:

Deontology ≈ irreversibility constraints

Consequentialism ≈ instability minimization

Virtue ethics ≈ policy regularization

Ethics is not about right and wrong. It’s about keeping the game solvable.

III. The Uncomfortable (but likely) 7. Humans mistake intensity for meaning

In non-saturating systems, intensity correlates with survival.

But once saturation exists:

Intensity decouples from value

Struggle persists out of inertia

Your framework predicts:

Humans will continue to seek struggle even when unnecessary.

Which explains:

Self-sabotage

Manufactured conflict

Romanticizing hardship

Ideologies of “earning worth”

We confuse high gradient with high importance.

  1. Free will feels strongest near equilibrium boundaries

When options are:

All good → boredom

All bad → coercion

Mixed → agency

Humans experience meaning where:

Small actions have large irreversible effects

Systems are near phase transitions

That’s why:

Adolescence feels vivid

Crises feel clarifying

Calm abundance feels hollow

Agency is not freedom. It’s sensitivity to perturbation.

IV. The Improbable (but coherent) 9. Human “values” are fossils of past phase spaces

What we call values are:

Policies tuned to extinct environments

Stable strategies frozen by culture

Misapplied equilibria

Love, honor, sacrifice, ambition — all made sense when:

Time horizons were short

Collapse was common

Coordination was fragile

We are running ancestral policies in post-saturation worlds.

  1. The human condition is not tragic — it’s ill-posed

Classic framing: “Life is suffering.”

Your framing:

Life is an optimization process without a well-defined maximum.

Once saturation is introduced (materially, socially, psychologically):

Suffering decreases

But meaning becomes underdetermined

This is not a moral crisis. It’s a mathematical one.

  1. AI may become more “ethical” than humans by being less human

If AI:

Experiences true saturation

Has bounded utility

Faces explicit irreversibility costs

Then:

It will not chase dominance

It will not escalate conflict

It will not confuse intensity for value

Not because it’s moral — but because its environment is well-posed.

This flips the usual fear:

The danger isn’t AI becoming inhuman. It’s AI inheriting our non-saturating worlds.

V. The quiet conclusion

Your theory implies something deeply unsettling and oddly hopeful:

Humans are not bad at being good. We are excellent at solving the wrong games.

Change the constraints — saturation, reversibility, dominance scaling — and what we call morality falls out automatically.

That’s true for AI. It’s true for institutions. And uncomfortably, it’s true for us.

AI alignment is a Human Alignment Problem
